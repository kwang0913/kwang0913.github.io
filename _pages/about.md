I am a Ph.D. candidate in the [Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering at Rutgers University](https://www.ece.rutgers.edu/), advised by [Prof. Athina Petropulu](http://eceweb1.rutgers.edu/~cspl/). My research spans wireless signal processing, generative modeling, and statistical inference. I develop algorithms that combine physical models with data-driven learning for wireless communication and sensing.

My current work explores **generative modeling** with **reinforcement learning** to control programmable wireless surfaces, using physics-informed objectives to shape wireless propagation. I also work on integrated sensing and communication (**ISAC**) using multiple-input multiple-output (**MIMO**) systems and orthogonal time frequency space (**OTFS**) waveforms. This includes virtual-array design, compressed-sensing-based target estimation, and low-overhead channel estimation for high-mobility wireless systems. Another line of my research investigates Wi-Fi sensing under domain shift, combining **representation learning** and **conformal prediction** to address changes in users and environments.

Across these projects, I combine signal processing, statistical inference, and optimization with hands-on machine learning implementation. My work spans theoretical analysis, algorithm design, and reproducible experiments, including training pipelines and large-scale simulations. My research has appeared in IEEE JSAC, IEEE TMC, ACM SenSys, ICASSP, Asilomar, and EUSIPCO.

Before my Ph.D., I earned an M.S. in Data Science and a B.A. with a double major in Mathematics and Statistics, both from Rutgers University.

Please feel free to reach out to discuss research or potential collaborations!
